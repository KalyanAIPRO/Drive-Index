# Commercial Licensing — Drive Index

Drive Index is **proprietary software**. `LICENSE` grants no rights by default;
all commercial rights are retained by the author. This file describes how a
third party obtains the right to use it, and how the author monetises it.

## 1. Why the licence is structured this way

| Model | Others may resell it | You can charge | Fits this repo |
|---|---|---|---|
| MIT / Apache-2.0 | **Yes** | Hard — anyone can fork and undercut you | No |
| PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0 | No (commercial use needs your licence) | Yes | Good, if you want a public free tier |
| Business Source License 1.1 | No, until the change date | Yes | Good, if you want eventual open source |
| **Proprietary — all rights reserved** | **No** | **Yes, on any terms you set** | **Yes — current choice** |

A permissive licence would let a competitor take this, wrap it, and sell it
against you. Proprietary keeps every commercial path open, and is the right
default while the repository is private.

**If you later want a public free tier**, replace `LICENSE` with the verbatim
text of one of:

- PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0 — <https://polyformproject.org/licenses/noncommercial/1.0.0/>
- Business Source License 1.1 — <https://mariadb.com/bsl11/>

Copy the official text from those URLs. Do not paraphrase a licence.

## 3. Other monetisation routes for this codebase

- **Google Workspace Marketplace** — publish as a paid or freemium editor
  add-on. Requires an OAuth verification review and a published privacy policy;
  `docs/PRIVACY.md` is the starting point for that.
- **Paid spreadsheet template** — sell a pre-wired Sheet (Gumroad, Lemon
  Squeezy, Payhip). Lowest friction; buyers never touch code.
- **Productised setup service** — fixed-fee install, configure and train,
  typically bundled with a Single Workspace licence.
- **Support and maintenance retainer** — priority fixes and feature requests,
  billed annually alongside the licence.

## 5. Not legal advice

These documents were drafted as engineering templates, not by a lawyer. Before
you take money against them — especially for the White-Label, OEM or Buy-Out
tiers, or if you sell into the EU, UK or US — have a lawyer review the licence
and your invoicing terms. Get advice on GST treatment of software licence
income before issuing an invoice.
